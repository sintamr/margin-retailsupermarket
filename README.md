<table align="center">

      <h2 align="center">Business Context & Problem Statement</h2>
      <body>
        <strong>Retail Supermarket</strong> manages thousands of daily transactions across five major product categories. Sales patterns show significant seasonal fluctuations, particularly during the Eid al-Fitr period, when demand surges before the holiday and drops sharply afterward, causing inventory imbalances and operational inefficiencies.<br>
        <br>
        The CFO noted that the gross profit margin dropped sharply in April–May 2024 despite relatively stable transaction counts. The data team was tasked with investigating the root cause of this decline and providing actionable recommendations.<br>
        <br>An in-depth analysis was conducted to evaluate performance across 2024 data. The key insights and recommendations focus on the following areas:
      </body>
      <h3>Northstar Metrics</h3>
      <h4>
        <ul>
          <li>Monthly gross margin trends - Analyzing the sharp drop during April-May 2024 and subsequent recovery.</li>
          <li>Category-specific margin impact - Evaluating performance across Beverages, Fresh Food, Frozen Food, Personal Care, and Snacks.</li>
          <li>Supply chain and inventory drivers - Monitoring order quantity vs. quantity sold, shrinkage rates, and reactive discounting.</li>
          <li>Supplier cost index - Assessing purchase price variations across seasonal periods.</li>
        </ul>
      </h4>
    </td>
  </tr>
</table>

<table align="center">
  <tr>
    <td width="460" valign="top">
      <ol>
        <li>
          <strong>Seasonal Margin Squeeze:</strong>
          <ul>
            <li>Gross profit margins dropped sharply in April (21%) and May (17%) 2024, down from the normal baseline of 23%.</li>
            <li>The margin compression lasted only 2 months before recovering fully to 23% in June, indicating a seasonal rather than structural issue.</li>
          </ul>
        </li>
        <li>
          <strong>Category-Specific Vulnerability:</strong>
          <ul>
            <li>Fresh Food was the only category affected, with its margin plummeting from 28% to 3.7% post-Eid.</li>
            <li>All other categories (Beverages, Frozen Food, Personal Care, and Snacks) remained completely stable.</li>
          </ul>
        </li>
      </ol>
    </td>
    <td width="460" valign="top">
      <ol start="3">
        <li>
          <strong>Inventory & Operational Drivers:</strong>
          <ul>
            <li>Post-Eid order volumes surged to 7.9 against actual sales of 3.1, creating a massive over-ordering gap.</li>
            <li>This overstock triggered a threefold increase in shrinkage (3.9% to 11.1%) and forced reactive discounting up to 19.8%.</li>
          </ul>
        </li>
        <li>
          <strong>Key Recommendations:</strong>
          <ul>
            <li>Implement demand-based ordering for fresh food using historical post-Eid data and shelf-life buffers.</li>
            <li>Cap reactive promotional discounts on fresh food at a maximum of 5%.</li>
            <li>Renegotiate supplier contracts to secure flat-rate or volume-flexible pricing for peak periods to counter supplier cost increases.</li>
          </ul>
        </li>
      </ol>
    </td>
  </tr>
</table>

<h1 align="center">Business Questions Addressed</h1>
<table align="center">
  <tr valign="top">
     <td width="900">
      <ul>
        <li><strong>What caused the decline?</strong> The gross margin drop was driven by a cascading failure in Fresh Food inventory management post-Eid: triple the shrinkage, heavy reactive markdowns, and an 8% supplier price hike.</li>
        <li><strong>Why did it happen?</strong> The purchasing team failed to adjust order quantities to match the post-holiday demand collapse, continuing to order at peak rates (7.9 avg) while actual sales dropped.</li>
        <li><strong>Is it business-wide?</strong> No, the issue is strictly category-specific to Fresh Food, while Beverages, Frozen Food, Personal Care, and Snacks remained completely stable.</li>
        <li><strong>How long did it last?</strong> The margin compression lasted only two months (April and May) before self-correcting back to the standard 23% baseline in June.</li>
      </ul>
    </td>
  </tr>
</table>

<table align="center">
    <h1>Recommendations</h1>
    <h4>Based on financial and operational insights, here are actionable items for management.</h4>
      <ul>
        <h3>Demand-Based Ordering for Fresh Food</h3>
        <li>Implement baseline order quantities driven by historical post-Eid sales data rather than emotional or static forecasting.</li>
          <ul><li>Incorporate automated buffer stock adjustments tied directly to the short shelf life of individual fresh food items.</li></ul>
        <h3>Cap Reactive Discounts</h3>
        <li>Establish a strict corporate policy limiting fresh food promotional markdowns to a maximum of 5%.</li>
          <ul><li>Require formal executive approval for any markdown exceptions exceeding this threshold to prevent unmanaged margin erosion.</li></ul>
        <h3>Renegotiate Supplier Contracts</h3>
        <li>Address the 8% post-Eid supplier price increase through proactive procurement negotiations.</li>
          <ul><li>Secure flat-rate pricing structures or volume-flexible contracts specifically tailored to cushion margins during seasonal demand shifts.</li></ul>
      </ul>
</table>
